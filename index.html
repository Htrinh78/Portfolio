About

I am a Biochemistry M.S. candidate at San Francisco State University with hands-on expertise in metalloenzyme characterization, membrane protein purification, and redox chemistry. My research focuses on integrating Styrene Oxide Isomerase (SOI) — a membrane-associated heme enzyme — into a cell-free biosynthetic pathway for penicillin production. I am experienced in site-directed mutagenesis, anaerobic UV-Vis spectroscopy, steady-state kinetics, and coupled enzyme assay design, with a passion for bridging enzymology and synthetic biology.

Research

Thesis Project — Integrating Styrene Oxide Isomerase into a Cell-Free Pathway for Penicillin Synthesis

Styrene oxide isomerase (SOI) is a membrane-associated heme enzyme that catalyzes the isomerization of styrene oxide to phenylacetaldehyde — a key step in a cell-free penicillin biosynthesis route. My thesis work characterizes the enzyme's electrochemical properties, active-site requirements, and functional integration with downstream biosynthetic enzymes.
